\paragraph{Graph filter banks and multiresolution analysis.}
Perfect-reconstruction graph filter banks predate this work. Graph-QMF couples a
two-channel spectral bank to graph down/up-sampling and derives alias-cancellation,
orthogonality, and perfect-reconstruction conditions on bipartite graphs; arbitrary
graphs are handled through bipartite subgraph decomposition~\cite{narang2012graphqmf}.
It is critically sampled. Tight GBDN instead retains $D+1$ full graph signals in its
complete depth-$D$ representation, so it is redundant and nonsubsampled. Undecimated
graph framelets are the closer precedent for this aspect: their full-resolution
multiscale coefficients form a tight representation with decomposition and
reconstruction~\cite{zheng2021framelets}. WaveGC further learns multiresolution graph
wavelets through an odd/even Chebyshev-order construction with wavelet-admissibility
constraints~\cite{liu2025wavegc}. GBDN therefore does not claim tight graph analysis,
perfect reconstruction, or multiresolution graph filtering as firsts. Its candidate
distinction is the learned Blaschke all-pass parameterization inside the complete
analysis map.

\paragraph{Polynomial and adaptive spectral filters.}
ChebNet realizes localized graph filters with sparse Chebyshev recurrences, and
ChebNetII parameterizes the response through Chebyshev interpolation
coefficients~\cite{defferrard2016chebnet,he2022chebnetii}. BernNet instead learns a
Bernstein-polynomial response and explicitly evaluates complicated band-rejection and
comb filters~\cite{he2021bernnet}. GPR-GNN learns signed generalized-PageRank
propagation weights~\cite{chien2021gprgnn}; UniFilter combines adaptive heterophily
and homophily polynomial bases~\cite{huang2024unifilter}; and HeroFilter adapts its
filter mixture after showing that the desired response need not vary monotonically
with a scalar heterophily level~\cite{zhang2025herofilter}. These methods prevent us
from describing GBDN as the first learnable or adaptive spectral graph filter. They
also motivate matched polynomial controls and caution against treating heterophily as
synonymous with high-frequency labels.

\paragraph{Rational and non-polynomial parameterizations.}
CayleyNets use complex Cayley polynomials. Both their coefficients and scale $h>0$
are optimized~\cite{levie2019cayleynets}. For one published real scalar response, the
uncancelled poles of the rational continuation are restricted to the shared pair
$\{ -i/h,+i/h\}$; the analytic half has the lower-axis pole $-i/h$. By contrast, each
admissible GBDN root selects an independent generic lower-half-plane pole of the exact
target. This is a pole-family distinction, not a claim that CayleyNets use fixed poles
or cannot form learned zeros. SLOG supplies another important boundary because it
constructs a real-valued adaptive non-polynomial spectral filter with an inductive
subgraph-sampling mechanism~\cite{xu2024slog}. GBDN consequently does not claim
rational, complex, adaptive, or non-polynomial graph filtering as new. Whether its
root geometry improves response fitting at a matched budget remains an experimental
question. Moreover, the deployed finite Chebyshev realization is polynomial and has
no literal poles; it approximates the exact movable-pole target.

\paragraph{Stability, depth, and transferability.}
Levie et al. establish linear stability and transferability for Cayley-smooth
spectral filters under graph perturbations~\cite{levie2019transferability}. Any GBDN
perturbation result must therefore be presented as a bound specialized to its exact
resolvent construction, not as the first graph-filter stability theorem. Unitary
Convolutions analyze same-dimensional unitary graph propagation and prove an
anti-oversmoothing result for that architecture~\cite{kiani2024unitary}. A-DGN derives
stable, non-dissipative antisymmetric dynamics~\cite{gravina2023adgn}, while
Stable-ChebNet casts high-order Chebyshev propagation as a stable, non-dissipative
dynamical system~\cite{hariri2025stablechebnet}. Those results concern propagated
states or dynamics. Tight GBDN's strongest unconditional statement concerns its
complete coefficient map, not the carried branch, nonlinear network, or
source-to-target sensitivity. Tightness alone therefore supports no oversquashing or
long-range claim.

\paragraph{Blaschke lineage and evaluation context.}
Classical PDU uses Blaschke decomposition for nonstationary signals
~\cite{coifman2025pdu}, and BDN turns related unwinding structure into a learned
product-sum architecture~\cite{zhang2025bdn}. Product-sum GBDN is BDN-inspired rather
than equivalent; the cited BDN record is a withdrawn ICLR submission. For evaluation,
the heterophily suite and fixed splits follow Platonov et
al.~\cite{platonov2023heterophily}. LRGB~\cite{dwivedi2022lrgb} remains a separate
graph-level protocol, with GraphGPS~\cite{rampasek2022graphgps} as upstream benchmark
infrastructure. These datasets motivate tests, not claims that GBDN already prevents
oversmoothing, relieves oversquashing, or solves long-range reasoning.
