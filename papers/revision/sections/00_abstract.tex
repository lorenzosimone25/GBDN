\begin{abstract}
Graph spectral filters often learn polynomial coefficients or coefficients in a
restricted rational family.  We instead learn the roots of Blaschke all-pass
factors after a Cayley transform of a self-adjoint graph operator.  Each root
controls localized phase geometry, while complementary half-sum and
half-difference channels convert phase into amplitude-selective analysis
bands.  We prove that the exact nonsubsampled multilevel analysis is an
isometry and give explicit additive and adjoint reconstruction recursions.  We
separate this exact target from its finite Chebyshev realization, for which we
derive frame-distortion, locality, and sparse-cost statements.  A cumulative
Product-sum extension admits finite-spectrum interpolation, while a relaxed
variant is treated separately without reconstruction guarantees.  Controlled
mechanism studies and a single-run legacy H100 reproduction are retained only
as diagnostics; comparative downstream claims require provenance-complete,
prespecified multi-run evaluation.
\end{abstract}
